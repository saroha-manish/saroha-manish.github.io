Multi-Carrier Provider FWA Risk-Ranking Model

An explainable machine learning solution that uses cross-carrier medical billing patterns to prioritize providers for fraud, waste, and abuse investigation.

Project Overview

Developed a provider-level fraud, waste, and abuse (FWA) risk-ranking model for auto casualty medical bills. The model analyzes five years of billing behavior across multiple insurance carriers and compares providers with historical cases flagged by the Special Investigations Unit (SIU).

The project used approximately 806 million bill lines across more than 90 client accounts. It built profiles for over 377,000 providers and scored approximately 298,000 previously unlabeled providers.

Each scored provider receives a risk probability, a review tier, and the 10 strongest reasons contributing to the score. Investigators use these outputs to decide which providers warrant closer examination.

Key Features

Cross-Carrier Provider View

Combines billing activity from multiple carriers to reveal provider patterns that may be difficult to see within one insurer's data.

Provider Risk Scoring

Assigns a model score to each provider based on historical billing behavior and its similarity to SIU-flagged providers.

Investigation Tiers

Organizes providers into High, Elevated, Low, and No Risk tiers to support investigation planning and worklist prioritization.

Explainable Reason Codes

Uses SHAP to identify the main billing features influencing each provider's score and gives investigators a starting point for review.

Behavioral Feature Engineering

Captures patterns in billing volume, pricing, procedure repetition, geography, attorney involvement, and timing.

Production Scoring Pipeline

Supports monthly feature rebuilding, provider scoring, run logging, model versioning, and automated output checks.

Controlled Model Promotion

Checks a candidate model against held-out performance before replacing the model currently used for scoring.

Technologies and Tools

Programming: Python
Data Platform: Snowflake, SQL, Snowflake Notebooks
Modeling: XGBoost, LightGBM, HistGradientBoosting, Random Forest, Logistic Regression
Explainability: SHAP-based provider reason codes
Feature Engineering: Provider-level billing, pricing, procedure, geographic, and timing measures
Model Evaluation: PR-AUC, ROC-AUC, top-decile hit rate, capture rate, and lift

Model Operations: Snowflake Model Registry, version aliases, run logging, and output quality checks

Problem Statement

SIU teams had to review a large provider population with limited investigative capacity. Suspicious billing could be identified only after substantial payments, while manual review offered no consistent way to decide which providers should be investigated first.

A provider may bill several insurance carriers. Looking at one carrier at a time can miss unusual patterns in the provider's overall activity. The business needed a cross-carrier risk signal that could focus investigators on promising leads while showing the billing behavior behind each recommendation.

Solution Approach

Built a supervised classification model at the provider Tax ID (TIN) level. TIN aligned with SIU case handling and provided more complete provider coverage than National Provider Identifier (NPI), which was missing for a substantial portion of the population.

  • Training Population: Used all 7,507 SIU-flagged providers and a stratified sample of 71,837 not-flagged providers. Sampling accounted for customer, provider type, and claim volume.
  • Label Quality: Applied additional exclusions for providers suspected of wrongdoing, including matches to public fraud and exclusion lists, to reduce contamination of the not-flagged group.
  • Feature Development: Created 203 behavioral features in 15 groups. Converted detailed billing codes into measures such as procedure concentration, diversity, and share of activity.
  • Feature Selection: Applied missing-value, constant-value, and collinearity checks. Tested 17 feature-set configurations and selected 104 features for the final model.
  • Model Selection: Compared five algorithm families and selected XGBoost. Tuning included a train-validation performance-gap constraint to reduce overfitting.
  • Investigator Output: Registered the model in Snowflake, scored unlabeled providers, assigned risk tiers, and stored SHAP-based reasons with the provider scores.

Results & Business Impact

  • PR-AUC: Achieved 0.758, compared with a 0.095 flagged-provider rate in the evaluation sample.
  • ROC-AUC: Achieved 0.951 on held-out data, indicating strong separation between flagged and not-flagged providers.
  • Top-Decile Hit Rate: 68.8% of providers in the highest-scoring 10% were SIU-flagged, equivalent to 7.3× lift over the sample baseline.
  • Top-Decile Capture: The highest-scoring 10% contained 72.8% of all SIU-flagged providers in the evaluation set.
  • Top-20% Capture: The highest-scoring 20% contained 89.5% of SIU-flagged providers.
  • Improvement Over Prior Model: PR-AUC increased from 0.719 to 0.758, an approximately 5.5% relative improvement.

Business Value

The model gives SIU teams an explainable, prioritized provider worklist. It helps investigators concentrate limited review capacity on providers whose billing patterns warrant closer examination across carriers.

Challenges

  • Incomplete Provider Identifiers: NPI was missing for approximately 33% of providers, so the model used TIN as the provider unit for training and investigation.
  • Uncertain Negative Labels: A provider without an SIU flag is not necessarily free of FWA. Additional exclusions reduced the risk of treating suspected providers as clean training examples.
  • Class Imbalance: The sampled not-flagged group was about 9.6 times the flagged group. Model development accounted for this imbalance during training and evaluation.
  • Sampling Bias: Stratified sampling helped represent different customers, provider types, and claim volumes without allowing sampling choices to become a model signal.
  • Cross-Carrier Data Quality: Addressed bill and claimant identifier collisions across customers and checked whether differences in carrier workflows could distort provider features.
  • Actionable Explanations: Used provider-level SHAP reasons so investigators could connect elevated scores to billing patterns visible in the underlying records.
Positioning Note: This model is an investigation-prioritization tool. An elevated score identifies a provider for closer review; it does not establish fraud. SIU investigators assess the underlying records and make final decisions.
